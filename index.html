Beginner guide

Test vs ODI vs T20: What is the difference?

Understand the three major international formats and what makes each one unique.

Test cricket: Traditionally played over up to five days, with each team having two innings. It rewards patience, technique and strategy.

One Day International (ODI): Each team normally gets 50 overs. The format combines longer innings with a single-day match.

Twenty20 (T20): Each team gets 20 overs, creating a shorter and often faster-paced contest.

Cricket basics

How does a cricket over work?

A simple explanation of legal deliveries, extras and how overs fit into an innings.

An over normally consists of six legal balls bowled by one bowler from one end. Certain deliveries, such as wides and no-balls, do not count as one of the six legal balls. The next over is bowled from the opposite end by a different bowler.

Player roles

What makes a great all-rounder?

Explore the skills needed to contribute with both bat and ball.

An all-rounder contributes meaningfully in more than one discipline, commonly batting and bowling. Teams value the balance an effective all-rounder can provide. Wicketkeeping, fielding and leadership are also important skills, but they do not alone define a batting-and-bowling all-rounder.

Cricket history

A quick introduction to cricket history

From early forms of the game to the international sport followed around the world.

Cricket developed in England over several centuries before spreading internationally. The first recognised men's Test match took place in 1877 between Australia and England. Limited-overs formats later brought new audiences and styles to the sport.
